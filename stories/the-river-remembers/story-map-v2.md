## POV Selection Rationale

- **Alexandria** is the primary POV. She is the entry character, the audience surrogate into the world of Salthaven and the network. Her arc from weapon to daughter is the heart of the volume. She witnesses the key revelations—the files, the serpent attack, the Storm Front fight, and the final battle. Her perspective carries the central dramatic irony (she knows more than either parent for much of the story).

- **Hark** provides the internal world of a man ruled by his rules, his slow thaw, his terror of hope. His POV is crucial for the mentorship's weight, the decision to break rules (serpent attack), the midpoint confession (receiving the truth), and the physical and emotional toll of combat (Team 3, the purge). His voice is distinct, terse, and sardonic.

- **Renne** offers the emotional core of grief and atonement. Her POV is used for the initial sighting (the impossible moment of seeing her daughter), the decision to tell Hark, and the network's leadership under pressure. Her scenes often involve the cost of healing and the weight of the past.

- **Secondary POVs** are used sparingly for specific functions:
  - **Kel:** to convey the unique auditory perception, his hostility toward Hark, and the moment his mutation burns out. His POV also provides external insight into Renne's sadness.
  - **Vara:** her single POV chapter marks the turning point—the moment an Order loyalist becomes a traitor.
  - **Marta and Doren:** their death chapters give them dignity and allow the reader to witness their sacrifices directly, adding emotional weight to the network's loss.

---

## Thematic Threads Woven Through the Map

- **The Dried Flower:** Appears in Renne's POV (Chapter 18), shown to Hark. Referenced again in the Epilogue.
- **Lea's Ghost:** Haunts Hark's POV during the serpent attack, and Alexandria's discovery in the files. The name "Lea" is spoken aloud only once, but echoes.
- **The Rules:** Hark's internal monologue across his POVs tracks each rule as it breaks.
- **Two Mercies:** The contrast between the Order's "mercy" (Vannis's scene, the purge) and the world's mercy (Renne's healing, the town's complicity, Cinnabar's turn).

---

This story map ensures that the slow-burn tension escalates to a devastating, action-packed climax while keeping the emotional truth of three broken people finding each other at the center. All perspectives serve the narrative, and no moment is seen through eyes that don't deepen the reader's understanding.
